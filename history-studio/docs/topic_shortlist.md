# Topic shortlist and scoring (2026-10-18)

## How demand was assessed, and its limits
- **Measured observations** come only from what was openly checkable on 2026-10-18 via web search and public pages:
  - A 2026 Marathi feature film about the Agra escape, *Ranapati Shivray: Swari Agra* (released 6 Feb 2026), received positive reviews but reportedly earned only about ₹1.3–1.4 crore net against an estimated ₹7 crore budget (Koimoi, Sacnilk via search).
  - Web search surfaced **no English animated treatment** of the Agra escape.
  - Major animated history channels (Simple History, Kings and Generals) exist with millions of subscribers, according to third-party trackers.
- **Not measured:** YouTube search volume, competitors' view counts by video, Google Trends.
  - YouTube's Terms of Service forbid automated scraping, so the pipeline does not scrape.
  - Google Trends has no public API for this use.
  - To add this data compliantly, set `YOUTUBE_API_KEY` (YouTube Data API v3, free quota) and/or check Trends manually in a browser.
- **The scores below are editorial judgments out of 100, not predictions.** No probability of virality is implied.

## Shortlist detail

**1. Shivaji at Agra (selected for episode 1)**
- **Audience and stakes:** India-first, with high recognition. A captive ruler, the empire's most dangerous rival, and his child's life at risk.
- **Sources:** The premise is firmly established by contemporary Rajasthani letters (1666, published by Sarkar), *Maasir-i-Alamgiri*, the Sabhasad chronicle and Manucci.
- **Recent coverage:** the 2026 Marathi film. Web search found no English animated version; YouTube view counts were not collected (see the limits above).
- **Distinctive angle:** legend vs letters. The baskets are attested within about two weeks; the long pilgrimage route was abandoned by Sarkar himself after the 1939 letters.
- **Animation:** court, fort, compound, baskets, maps. Medium difficulty. One episode.

**2. Bligh (1789)**
- **Audience and stakes:** global. Navigating 3,600 nautical miles with almost no instruments.
- **Sources:** Bligh's own published narrative (public domain).
- **Angle:** the navigation itself, shown on a map day by day.
- **Animation:** ocean, boat, map. Low to medium difficulty. One episode.

**4. Netaji's Great Escape (1941)**
- **Audience and stakes:** India-first, with high recognition. A disguised escape under police watch.
- **Sources:** scholarship exists (Sugata Bose, Leonard Gordon; Sisir Kumar Bose's account), but these are in-copyright books that need library access. The pipeline must open them before scripting.
- **Format:** two parts (Calcutta → Kabul; Kabul → Berlin), planned before part 1 is produced.

**5, 9 (timely):** the balloon escape (near 9 Nov, the anniversary of the fall of the Berlin Wall) and Silkyara (12 Nov, the anniversary of the collapse). Silkyara involves living people and needs respectful, news-sourced treatment.

**12. Lachit Borphukan:** a natural follow-on, because the episode 1 ending honestly bridges to Ram Singh's defeat at Saraighat.
