In 1666, Shivaji walked into Aurangzeb's court as a guest and became a prisoner. Three months later, guarded by what one letter calls a thousand men, he and his nine-year-old son vanished. The famous story says baskets of sweets. Letters written in Agra that summer let us check the legend against the record.

This animated history explains why Shivaji went to Agra after the Treaty of Purandar (1665), what happened at the audience of 12 May 1666, how his visit turned into captivity, and how the escape in the third week of August 1666 worked, according to contemporary Rajasthani letters, the Mughal court history Maasir-i-Alamgiri, the Maratha chronicle Sabhasad Bakhar and the memoir of Niccolao Manucci. We also look at what later legend added, what the letters found in 1939 changed, and what the escape meant for the Deccan.

What the sources say (and don't)
• Sources disagree on the exact date: a letter from Agra dated 18 August 1666 says Shivaji was found gone 'this morning'; Maasir-i-Alamgiri gives Sunday 19 August.
• The decoy (Hiroji Farzand) and Sambhaji's stay in Mathura come from later Maratha accounts and are presented as such.
• Dialogue quoted on screen comes from English translations of contemporary letters (J. Sarkar); no dialogue was invented.
• Animation is an illustrated reconstruction, not a likeness of any historical person.
