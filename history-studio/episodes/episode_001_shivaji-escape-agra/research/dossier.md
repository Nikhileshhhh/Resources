## Verified core (contemporary documents)
- **12 May 1666:** audience on Aurangzeb's 50th lunar birthday. The emperor did not speak to Shivaji. Robes went to the princes, Jafar Khan and Jaswant Singh. Shivaji said "I cast off your mansab" and walked away (S1; date also S4).
- Court pressure (Jahanara, Jafar Khan, Jaswant Singh). The emperor weighed killing or confining him. Ram Singh offered to die first and signed a **security bond** (S1).
- A Kabul posting was ordered, then rescinded after petitions and payments. Guards from Fulad Khan's force and Ram Singh's own Rajputs kept watch (S1, S2).
- Petitions: two crore rupees for his forts, to fight Bijapur, another residence, to go to Benares as a mendicant. Reply: "Let him turn faqir and live in Allahabad fort" (S1).
- **c. July:** Jai Singh advised keeping Shivaji at Agra, "not at all politic" to let him return (S1, S2). Shivaji borrowed Rs 66,000 from Ram Singh (S1 note). His escort left about 25 July (S1 note).
- **Escape:** letter dated **18 Aug 1666**: "This morning Shivaji was found to have fled" (S1). The *Maasir* gives **Sunday 19 August** (S4). A letter of **3 Sept**: "afterwards it became known that he had slipped out by crouching down in the baskets" (S1).
- **Aftermath:** Ram Singh's men were threatened into "confessing" collusion; some recanted. Ram Singh's rank was cut and his lands taken (S1, S4). Spy reports say Shivaji reached his fort in **25 days** (≈12 Sept) and lay ill for weeks (S1).

## Names, pronunciation and geography
- **Rajgad** (Rajgarh, 1666 home base) is not **Raigad** (coronation capital, 1674). Both are labelled explicitly on screen.
- Spellings follow modern common usage (Aurangzeb, Sambhaji). Pronunciation respellings and their whisper-check results are in `pronunciations.json`.
- Distances: Agra–Rajgad is about 1,088 km in a straight line (great-circle computation). The narration says "more than a thousand kilometres".
- Maps use the modern Natural Earth coastline and rivers with **no historical borders**; this simplification is stated on screen.

## Costume and architecture notes
- Agra Fort: red sandstone walls; white-marble halls inside. The letters place the audience in the **Diwan-i-Khas**, not the Diwan-i-Am as Sarkar wrote in 1920. The hall is shown as a marble arcade.
- Shivaji: per the 1666 eyewitness, "lean and short… fair… wears a beard". His flag is "orange and vermilion".
- Characters are stylised, not likenesses. Jahanara appears only in silhouette.

## Sensitivity
The story is often told in communal terms. The script narrates documented actions on both sides (including Jai Singh's harsh July letter) without assigning religious motives, and quotes hostile language from sources only where it is attributed ("the wretch Shiva", from a collection attributed to Aurangzeb).
